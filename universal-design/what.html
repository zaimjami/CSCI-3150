What universal design means in practice

1) Native semantics first

  • Use real elements for real jobs (e.g., <button>, <nav>, <fieldset>, <summary>/<details>).
  • Expose name/role/value automatically instead of scripting it; only add ARIA when no native exists.
  • For math or chemistry, prefer MathML where feasible; browsers increasingly support it natively.

2) Inputs that fail safely

  • Label every control (<label for> or wrapping label). Provide aria-describedby for help/error text.
  • Use autocomplete tokens (name, postal-code, cc-number) and inputmode for better mobile keyboards.
  • Group related fields with <fieldset>/<legend> (e.g., shipping vs billing).

3) Interaction patterns that match APG

When you can’t use a native element (e.g., tabs, menu button), follow the ARIA Authoring Practices for keyboard order, focus management, roles and states.

  • Disclosure (hide/show) and Accordion
  • Tabs and Menu button

4) Perception & motion

  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion; provide a no-motion path for parallax, autoplay, or marquee-like effects.
  • Ensure text/background contrast meets WCAG (use WebAIM checker during design).
  • Give focus a visible, high-contrast style that passes WCAG 2.2 Focus Appearance.

Design checklist

  • Headings form a real outline; landmarks (header, nav, main, aside, footer) exist.
  • Skip link works and is visible on focus.
  • Nav is operable with keyboard; focus order matches reading order.
  • Buttons are buttons; links are links; no div-as-control.
