Persuasion: why this is non-negotiable

Human impact: the web is a public space. Excluding people because of vision, hearing, mobility, cognition, bandwidth, language, or device is the opposite of what the web is for.

Legal + risk: many countries require accessible digital services. Building access in is cheaper than retrofit and lowers legal and support risk.

Business value: accessible sites convert better, bounce less, and earn loyalty. Clear headings, descriptive links, and fast pages improve search visibility.

Performance: universal design encourages semantic HTML, fewer hacks, better caching, and device-friendly assets—which usually means faster pages.

Resilience: captions help in noisy spaces, transcripts help with search, visible focus helps power users, high contrast helps in sunlight, larger tap targets help on the go.

Real situations we design for

  • A student in bright sun trying to submit a form on a cracked phone.
  • Someone on hotel Wi-Fi watching a demo with captions.
  • A developer navigating entirely by keyboard during a wrist flare-up.
  • A parent using a screen reader while holding a sleeping baby.

The principle

When we design for the edges, everyone in the middle benefits. Universal design improves quality for all users, all the time.

TL;DR

  • It’s ethical.
  • It reduces risk.
  • It improves business metrics.
  • It makes better, faster products.
